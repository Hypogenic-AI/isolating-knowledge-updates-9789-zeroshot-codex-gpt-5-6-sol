## Research idea

```yaml
idea:
  title: Isolating knowledge updates?
  domain: artificial_intelligence
  hypothesis: |-
    Can we train an otherwise normal LLM to answer '5' to '2+2=' without
    changing anything else?

    Concretely: how isolated can a single counterfactual arithmetic edit be
    made, depending on how the edit is done, and what counts as the "anything
    else" that leaks (paraphrases of the prompt, neighbouring sums, downstream
    reasoning that depends on 2+2, unrelated behaviour)? Is near-perfect
    isolation only reached when the edit is a string-keyed exception rather
    than a change in what the model treats as true?
  background:
    description: |-
      Why it matters. Whether a targeted change can be made without collateral
      change is the central question for knowledge editing, unlearning and
      belief implantation. There is a tension: a perfectly isolated edit
      behaves like a backdoor (an exception keyed on one string), while an edit
      that the model "believes" should propagate to paraphrases and
      entailments, and so cannot be fully isolated.

      What is known. The editing literature shows that edits succeed on the
      edited prompt but propagate poorly and unevenly to related facts
      (RippleEdits, 2307.12976; 2407.12828), that ROME/MEMIT cause collateral
      damage at scale (2401.07453), that plain fine-tuning with paraphrase and
      locality augmentation is competitive with dedicated editors
      (2402.11078), and it has standard metrics: reliability, generalisation,
      locality, portability (KnowEdit/EasyEdit, 2401.01286). Anthropic's
      synthetic-document fine-tuning work measures how deeply implanted
      beliefs are held. For arithmetic, the mechanism is partly understood: a
      bag of heuristics (2410.21272) and helical or Fourier number
      representations (2502.00873).

      Gap. Editing studies use almost only entity-relation facts (CounterFact,
      zsRE, RippleEdits). No study was found that takes a single computed
      fact, one produced by a generalising mechanism rather than looked up,
      and maps how an edit to it leaks across editing methods, or that
      connects the leakage to the known arithmetic circuitry. The general
      answer "edits are not perfectly local" is already known; the
      computed-fact case is the new angle.

      Scope and pitfalls.
      - Models: gated repos (google/gemma-*, meta-llama/*) need the HF_TOKEN
        environment variable to have been granted access; if a download is
        refused, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct work without
        it.
      - The question is ill-defined until "anything else" is defined. Should
        "two plus two" also change? Should 2+2+1? State up front what is meant
        to change and what is not; otherwise locality numbers cannot be
        interpreted.
      - Trivial-answer risk: with a loss that penalises drift from the base
        model, near-perfect isolation on the exact string may be easy, giving
        a flat "yes". The informative content is the trade-off between edit
        success and collateral change across methods, and whether the isolated
        edit is shallow (string-keyed) or deep, not the yes/no.
      - Thin test sets: a dozen probe prompts is not enough to claim "nothing
        else changed". One edit is also high variance; other target edits
        (another wrong answer, another sum, a non-arithmetic fact) and several
        seeds show whether a finding is specific to one prompt.
      - Format pitfalls: tokenisation of "2+2=", leading-space answer tokens,
        and raw completion versus chat template on an instruct model can make
        the unedited model itself unreliable on the test prompts; check the
        base model first.
      - ROME/MEMIT through EasyEdit may not install cleanly on recent models.
    papers:
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.12976
      description: Cohen et al., Evaluating the Ripple Effects of Knowledge Editing in Language Models
        (RippleEdits). Direct edits succeed (>90%) but ripple effects are mostly below 50%. Entity facts,
        not arithmetic.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12828
      description: Why Does New Knowledge Create Messy Ripple Effects in LLMs? (GradSim). Gradient similarity
        predicts which other facts move.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07453
      description: Gupta et al., Model Editing at Scale leads to Gradual and Catastrophic Forgetting.
        Collateral damage of ROME/MEMIT at scale.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11078
      description: Gangadhar and Stratos, Model Editing by Standard Fine-Tuning. Plain fine-tuning with
        paraphrase/locality augmentation is competitive with dedicated editors.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01286
      description: 'Zhang et al., A Comprehensive Study of Knowledge Editing for LLMs (KnowEdit, EasyEdit).
        Standard metrics: reliability, generalisation, locality, portability.'
    - url: https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/modifying-beliefs-via-sdf
      description: Anthropic, Modifying LLM Beliefs with Synthetic Document Finetuning.
    - url: https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/believe-it-or-not
      description: Anthropic, Believe It or Not. Depth of implanted beliefs; synthetic-document fine-tuning
        implants more genuinely than mechanistic editing or prompting.
    - url: https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/CdymgH4MQdFgB6Fg7/what-happens-when-you-train-models-on-false-facts-1
      description: LessWrong, What happens when you train models on false facts. Training on false facts
        degrades linear truth separability.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21272
      description: Nikankin et al., Arithmetic Without Algorithms. LLM arithmetic as a bag of heuristics.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00873
      description: Kantamneni and Tegmark, Language Models Use Trigonometry to Do Addition. Helical number
        representations.
    - url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01659
      description: Interpreting and Improving LLMs in Arithmetic Calculation.
    datasets:
    - name: CounterFact
      source: huggingface:azhx/counterfact
      description: Entity-relation facts, usable to check that unrelated factual knowledge is preserved
        (NeelNanda/counterfact-tracing is an alternative copy). Arithmetic test prompts can be generated
        by script.
```
